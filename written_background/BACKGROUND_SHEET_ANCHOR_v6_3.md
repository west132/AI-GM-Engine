Lara Croft, Nathan Drake with Sully, and Asian Hawk are all after it for their own reasons, alongside a London smuggling syndicate, a broke prince, a fringe professor and the family that has guarded the jewel for two thousand years. Governments are far away, police are for sale, and the only walls are monsoon floods and curfew cordons.

### For players / 玩家须知 (no spoilers)

India, July 1991. The country is about to open its economy; foreign money is coming in, and so is everyone who smells a chance. In Patna, an archaeologist has just been robbed of a copper plate dug from the old Mauryan capital — a plate in two scripts that, some say, proves the history books wrong by a thousand years. In Bombay, a fence in the thieves' market is quietly inviting bidders. And in some circles, people are using a word nobody has said seriously in centuries: *Syamantaka*.

The monsoon is coming. So are the treasure hunters.

1991年7月的印度。这个国家即将开放经济，外国资金涌入，嗅到机会的人也一起涌入。在巴特那，一位考古学家刚刚被偷走了一块铜板——它出土于古孔雀王朝的都城，刻着两种文字，有人说它能证明史书错了一千年。在孟买的贼市，一个销赃商正悄悄邀请买家。而在某些圈子里，人们重新认真地说起一个几百年没人当真的词：*夏曼多卡宝石*。
季风要来了。寻宝的人也来了。

### Real history and legend used here (GM and player-safe)

```text
REAL     Sir William Jones founded the Asiatic Society in Calcutta (1784) and in 1793
  matched the Greeks' "Sandrokottos" to Chandragupta Maurya — the "sheet anchor" of
  ancient Indian dates; Megasthenes, envoy of the Greek king Seleucus, lived at
  Pataliputra and wrote the Indica, now lost except in quotations; James Prinsep
  deciphered Brahmi in 1837; Ashoka's Rock Edict XIII names Greek kings (Antiochus,
  Ptolemy and others), which is how scholars check the anchor; a Greek–Aramaic edict of
  Ashoka was found at Kandahar in 1958; Mauryan Pataliputra's pillared hall was dug at
  Kumrahar, Patna, early in the 20th century; Rajasthan's stepwells go down a dozen
  storeys (Chand Baori at Abhaneri the famous one); Ellora's Kailasa temple is cut
  from one rock; in 1991 Bombay, Calcutta and Madras still have those names
LEGEND   the Syamantaka — given to King Satrajit by the sun god; it made gold every day
  and blessed a good keeper and ruined a bad one; Satrajit's brother Prasena wore it
  hunting and was killed by a lion; the bear-king Jambavan took it to his cave; Krishna
  fought Jambavan for weeks and won the jewel and Jambavan's daughter; some say the
  Koh-i-Noor is the Syamantaka · Vikramaditya's throne of thirty-two speaking statues,
  each telling a tale before it lets a king sit · the Nine Unknown Men, guardians of
  Ashoka's secret knowledge (from a 1923 novel, believed by some anyway) · Chandragupta
  Maurya ended his days as a Jain monk at Shravanabelagola, by Jain tradition
DEBATE   a fringe school says the Greeks met Chandragupta of the Gupta dynasty, not the
  Maurya, which would push Indian dates back over a thousand years; mainstream
  scholars reject it (Ashoka's edicts name the right Greek kings); in this world, the
  plate gives both camps something to shout about
```

locked_case_truths:
  plate:
    cause: >-
      The plate is genuine and old — but it is a copy. The original was a Mauryan grant made at Pataliputra in two
      scripts, Greek by a Greek scribe at court, Brahmi by an Indian one. Around AD 400, in the reign of Chandragupta
      II of the Gupta dynasty (whom legend made "Vikramaditya"), the temple family that guarded the jewel had the
      decaying original copied onto a new plate. The copyist reproduced the Greek letters faithfully without reading
      them, so the Greek line still names Sandrokottos — Chandragupta Maurya. In the Brahmi he wrote his own king's
      name and titles. Read in Greek, the plate supports the mainstream dates; read in Brahmi, it seems to support
      the long chronology; read in both, by someone who knows Gupta copying practice, it proves nothing about dates
      at all. Its other lines are a guardian's instructions in three steps: the throne that speaks (Vikramaditya's
      thirty-two statues, which the Gupta copyist added in praise of his king), the well of a thousand steps, and
      the cave of the bear-king.
    prior_events:
      - Found at Kumrahar, Patna, in June 1991 by Dr Meera Raghavan's dig, in a later fill above the Mauryan floor.
      - Day −2 — taken from Meera's lab safe (lab_theft).

## Setting notes (GM-facing)

- **Opening.** Day 0, morning. Routine continuation (engine §16.2 step 7) to the role's first contact — archaeologist: Day 0, Meera asks the PC to help find the plate · treasure hunter: Day 1, Bakshi's runner brings an invitation to the auction · pilot: Day 1, Sully tries to hire the Dakota for "a quick getaway, Day 2" · stunt performer: Day 2 night, the auction brawl crashes through the wall of the set · fixer: Day 0, Bakshi calls in a panic — too many bidders, too many guns. No invented errand or NPC question fills the gap.
- **If the PC does nothing.** The auction (Day 2) ends in a brawl; the plate snaps — Drake and Sully run with the Brahmi half, Kasteel's men with the Greek half; Lara has photographs of both faces. Iyengar goes to the papers (Day 3). Hartog reads the Greek and the throne (Day 4). Lara reads her photographs (Day 4). Sully sells to Sterne, Drake double-crosses him (Day 5–6). Devgarh fills up (Day 6–7). Sterne's clock and the monsoon run. Whoever reads the replica throne goes to the wrong valley first. Every step needs its actor's means and time; play can change any of them.
- **The two halves.** From Day 2 the plate is two objects. The Greek half carries the Sandrokottos line and the throne instruction; the Brahmi half carries the Gupta king's line, the well and the cave. Lara's photographs hold both faces but are hard to read in the Gupta letters. Keep exact who holds what.
- **Stars are people.** Lara, Drake, Sully and Hawk follow their own plans and can win. They help the PC when it suits them, banter with them, compete with them, and can be bought, tricked, outclimbed or out-thought. Never make one a fool to make the PC look good, or a rescuer to save the PC.
- **Stunt comedy.** Every fight has props and a place: a stack of film reels, a tea stall, a bicycle with no brakes, the plywood palace set. Hawk especially fights with everything in the room and apologises. Comedy sits beside real danger.
